Deriving and testing scaling rules.

01

The best scaling rule depends on the training setting.

216 rules in language modeling. 648 on CIFAR-5M. The best common prescription differs, and the language-model winner also changes with the target batch.

Larger batches can increase training throughput, but retaining optimization performance is challenging and often requires adjusting hyperparameters. Throughout the years, multiple empirical and theoretical research programs have repeatedly investigated the correct "hyperparameter scaling rule" with respect to batch size. Can any of these prescriptions, or alternatives beyond them, can fulfill this promise:

Q1: Can a hyperparameter scaling rule consistently work in different training settings?

We summarize and derive scaling rules for MuonW-AdamW hybrid. We then compare them with a broad grid of candidate rules, combinatorially exhausting possible scaling rules for all hyperparameters, resulting in 216 possible scaling rules. Unfortunately, we found the optimal rule depends on the setting and target batch size. In language modeling, both the SDE invariant rule and convex bound minimization rule both fall behind the trivial baseline of keeping constant learning rate.

Concretely, we train and tune a reference model in two settings: language modeling (following modded nanogpt speedrun track 3) at 128K tokens batch size, and CIFAR-5M image classification with ResNet with Group Norm at 256 image batch size. We then increase the batch size and test different ways of adjusting the training settings. We will denote the new batch / reference batch as batch size ratio κ.

Matching an SDE

Linear learning rate.

ηM′=κηM,μ′=μκ,λM′=λM

The idealized SDE calculation holds the coefficient mixing gradients and momentum fixed. MuonW ties this coefficient to momentum, so exact invariance is not guaranteed.

Minimizing a bound

Square-root learning rate.

ηM′=κηM,μ′=μ,λM′=λM

Balances the initialization and smoothness terms in a stationarity bound, until its effective step-size cap becomes active.

Power Lines

Fixed learning rate.

ηM′=ηM,μ′=μ,λM′=κλM

Preserves the nominal parameter-averaging timescale at a fixed token budget. We extend the same identity to MuonW with fixed momentum.

Curvature and gradient noise.

03

Curvature and noise shape batch-size effects.

Sharp directions limit SGD’s learning rate. Newton removes that constraint but rescales the noise.

Think of loss as height above the bottom of a bowl. This bowl is flat and noisy in one direction, steep and noiseless in the other. SGD follows the estimated slope; Newton adjusts each direction’s step for its curvature, or steepness.

SGDnew position = position − learning rate × estimated slope

Newtonnew position = position − learning rate × estimated slope / curvature

Why can the winner change?

In this bowl, SGD wins at batch 1, while Newton wins when all 4,096 samples form one batch. The steep direction limits SGD’s step size. Newton corrects for that steepness, allowing both directions to move together. Each method gets its own tuned learning rate.

Open the hood: the model and the tuning

The objective is L(w)=12(w12+h2w22). Gradients are g^i=hiwi+ciBzi, with independent standard Gaussian noise at every step. Here h1=1, c1 is the noise slider, and c2=0. The sample budget is T=4,096 and the number of updates is K=TB. Both methods start at the same point and use the same underlying noise draws.

SGD uses wi←wi−ηg^i. Newton uses wi←wi−ηhig^i. The learning rate is constant within each run. The canvas shows original parameter coordinates.

We search 701 logarithmically spaced learning rates in the stable interval and refine locally. The tuning budget is 4,096 samples. The dashed 4K marker on the loss plot marks that budget; continuation uses the same learning rates and noise process. With noise and a constant learning rate, trajectories settle into fluctuations around the minimum. Animation extends for at least 192 updates and four comparison budgets, with a 32,768-update limit. This is a numerical illustration of the mechanism in Section 5, not the paper’s theorem construction or a proof that every landscape has a crossover.

For every sufficiently large sample budget, there exists a two-dimensional NQM on which optimally tuned SGD outperforms optimally tuned Newton at batch 1, but the ranking reverses when the whole budget forms one batch. The theorem holds both without momentum and with optimally tuned momentum. The displayed no-momentum playground is a separate numerical illustration. The construction establishes that directional differences in the bias–variance tradeoff can reverse optimizer rankings even after optimal tuning.

The boundary changes with the starting point. The winner depends on the error left to correct, the noise in each update, and the number of updates available. The same batch size can therefore favor different methods in different setups.

Directional scaling rules.

04

Higher curvature-to-noise ratios shift learning rates toward linear scaling.

In SignSGD, a noisy direction benefits from more reliable signs. A direction whose sign is already reliable mostly gets fewer updates.

In the noisy quadratic setting, ideal Muon orthogonalization on a 1 × 1 block is the sign operation, motivating this SignSGD comparison. Curvature-to-noise ratio is squared curvature divided by single-example gradient-noise variance: CNR=h2c.

Momentum is fixed at μ=0.9. Curvature is h=1, single-example noise variance is c=1/CNR, initialization is w0∼N(0,1), and initial momentum is zero. The recursion applies the sign of momentum before each parameter update, without weight decay. For each batch, the search minimizes expected terminal loss after the fixed 4,096-sample budget.

The paper uses 121 broad logarithmic learning-rate candidates with 512 paths, refines three separated neighborhoods, selects with 2,048 independent paths, and evaluates on eight held-out groups of 1,024 paths. The fitted exponent is an ordinary least-squares slope of log learning rate against log batch over the nine tested batches. It summarizes those endpoints rather than asserting a universal power law.

Where do these curves come from?

Throughout the blog, B is a batch size before scaling, B′ the scaled batch, and κ=B′B their ratio. η is a learning rate, μ a momentum coefficient, and α a learning-rate exponent. Both directions use w=1,μ=0.9.

Assume independent Gaussian gradient noise. Freeze the coordinate w and let the momentum distribution settle to its steady state; this is stationary momentum. Let μ be the momentum coefficient and κ=B′B the batch ratio. The expected sign is A(B)=erf(wCNR·B(1+μ)2(1−μ)). Relative movement per processed sample is κα−1A(κB)A(B). We use reference B=1. Noise-dominated response grows approximately as B; signal-dominated response saturates. Preserving this local movement motivates square-root or linear scaling in the respective limits. The approximation freezes the parameter and assumes stationary momentum; it does not guarantee full-trajectory invariance. The best-compromise button minimizes the largest multiplicative deviation across the two directions, at the slider’s 0.01 resolution. See Section 5 and Appendix C of the paper.

The sharp-subspace intervention.

05

The sharpest 0.001% of directions remove up to 59.5% of the local penalty.

Keep a tiny selected subspace at small batch after a 16× batch increase. A random subspace of the same size changes the penalty by at most 3%.

A checkpoint is a saved training state. We copy it and continue training with different batch-size choices, giving every branch the same starting point and amount of further data.

A direction is a way of changing many weights together. We ask whether a few selected directions can retain the benefit of small-batch training:

  • 128K control: Small-batch updates everywhere.
  • Fully scaled to 2M: Large-batch updates everywhere.
  • Top-k: Small-batch updates in the k sharpest directions, large-batch updates elsewhere.
  • Random 768: Small-batch updates in 768 randomly chosen directions.

At early and middle checkpoints, switching everything to 2M leaves a higher loss than the 128K control. Keeping sharp directions at small batch brings the result closer to the control. These are directions where the loss bends most sharply, like the steep side of the bowl above.

Directional critical batch size asks when larger batches stop giving proportional reductions in the updates needed for progress in a particular direction. These branches test directional sensitivity; they do not estimate that threshold.

The bars measure the extra loss left by each branch relative to its small-batch control:

penalty = branch loss − small-batch control loss

Recovery measures how much of the fully scaled penalty is removed:

recovery = penalty removed / fully scaled penalty

Protocol, controls, and what this does not establish

The sharp directions have the largest eigenvalues of a Muon-preconditioned Gauss–Newton approximation to the loss Hessian, a local curvature proxy. Each continuation trains for 1,024 base steps, about 134M tokens. The penalty subtracts its small-batch control’s final validation loss.

The held subspace stays fixed throughout the continuation. Both Muon copies receive the same stream of base-batch gradients. Every 16 steps, the large-batch copy uses their mean gradient and 16 times their mean learning rate. Matrix weight decay and auxiliary AdamW updates stay on the base clock. This is a diagnostic intervention, not an ordinary large-batch training run.

Endpoints use 10.5M held-out tokens. Most branches are single runs, so these percentages are descriptive measurements, not significance estimates. The experiment selects directions by curvature and does not measure the full direction-dependent noise covariance. Its effect largely disappears near the end of training. We therefore cannot infer a 59.5% improvement in final loss, total training cost, or wall-clock speed.

Recovery is substantial through the middle of training but falls close to zero near the end. These results measure short continuations from saved checkpoints. Whether the same idea can improve a complete training run remains open.
